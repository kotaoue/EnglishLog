## テーマ: The Hedonic Treadmill and the Psychology of Happiness（ヘドニック・トレッドミルと幸福の心理学）

### 英文パッセージ

The hedonic treadmill, also known as hedonic adaptation, is a psychological concept that describes the human tendency to return to a stable level of happiness despite major positive or negative life changes. According to this theory, as a person makes more money or achieves their goals, their expectations and desires rise in tandem. This results in no permanent gain in overall happiness, as the individual quickly becomes accustomed to their new lifestyle.

For example, imagine someone who wins a large lottery prize. Initially, they experience a surge of intense joy. They may buy a new house, travel the world, and enjoy luxury items. However, after a few months, these new comforts become the "new normal." The excitement fades, and the individual’s happiness level eventually drifts back to where it was before the win. The same principle often applies to negative events, such as a physical injury; while devastating at first, many people eventually adapt and regain their baseline level of well-being over time.

Psychologists suggest that our "happiness set point" is determined by a combination of genetics and personality. While external achievements provide temporary boosts, they rarely lead to long-term fulfillment. To escape the treadmill, experts recommend focusing on "intrinsic" goals, such as building meaningful relationships, practicing gratitude, and engaging in personal growth, rather than chasing extrinsic rewards like wealth or status.
