## テーマ: The Concept of "Cognitive Liberty" in the Era of Neurotechnology

### 英文パッセージ

Cognitive liberty, often heralded as the final frontier of human rights, encompasses the right to self-determination over one’s own mind and mental states. As neurotechnological advancements—ranging from sophisticated brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) to non-invasive neuroimaging—accelerate, the sanctity of the internal psyche is increasingly under threat. Traditionally, legal frameworks have focused on protecting bodily integrity and external behaviors, yet the burgeoning ability to decode neural correlates of thought necessitates a paradigm shift in our conceptualization of privacy.

The core of cognitive liberty lies in two fundamental principles: the right to use emerging technologies to alter or enhance one’s own consciousness, and conversely, the right to refuse such interventions. This includes protection against the coercive use of neurotechnology and the unauthorized surveillance of brain data. In a future where "brain-mining" could potentially allow corporations or governments to monitor subconscious biases or emotional responses, the absence of robust "neurorights" could lead to an unprecedented form of digital hegemony.

Critics argue that current data protection laws are ill-equipped to handle the granularity and intimacy of neural information. Unlike metadata from internet searches, neural data is a direct reflection of the self, often bypassing conscious volition. Therefore, advocates for cognitive liberty suggest that we must treat the mind as an inalienable domain, distinct from traditional property or privacy categories. Integrating these protections into international human rights law is not merely a proactive measure; it is an essential safeguard against the encroaching commodification of human thought.
