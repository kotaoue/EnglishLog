## テーマ: The Fermi Paradox and the "Great Filter" Hypothesis

### 英文パッセージ

The Fermi Paradox highlights the startling discrepancy between the high statistical probability of extraterrestrial civilizations and the total lack of evidence for their existence. Given the billions of Sun-like stars in our galaxy, many of which are billions of years older than Earth, it stands to reason that advanced civilizations should have emerged and colonized the Milky Way by now. Yet, the "Great Silence" persists. To reconcile this, the "Great Filter" hypothesis suggests that there exists a formidable barrier in the evolutionary timeline of life that is nearly impossible to surpass.

This hypothetical filter could be positioned in our past or our future. If the filter lies behind us—perhaps in the abiogenesis of life or the transition from prokaryotic to eukaryotic cells—then humanity might be one of the few, or even the only, species to have successfully navigated it. This would imply that life is exceedingly rare, making our survival a cosmic fluke. Conversely, if the filter lies ahead of us, it suggests that most civilizations reach a certain level of technological prowess only to inevitably succumb to self-destruction, whether through nuclear cataclysm, runaway climate change, or uncontrollable artificial intelligence.

Under this framework, the discovery of complex life on Mars or Europa would be catastrophic news for humanity. It would suggest that the Great Filter is not the emergence of life itself, but something that occurs later in a civilization’s development. The more common life is in the universe, the more likely it is that we have yet to face our most daunting existential hurdle.
