## テーマ: The Pomodoro Technique and Productivity

### 英文パッセージ

Do you ever feel like you have too much to do and not enough time? Many students and professionals feel overwhelmed by their daily responsibilities. To solve this problem, many people use a time management method called the Pomodoro Technique. It was developed by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s. He named the technique after a tomato-shaped kitchen timer he used as a university student, as "pomodoro" is the Italian word for tomato.

The method is straightforward. First, pick a single task to focus on. Set a timer for 25 minutes and work intensely on that task until the timer rings. During this period, you must avoid all distractions, such as social media notifications or unimportant phone calls. Each 25-minute block is called one "Pomodoro."

Once the session is finished, you should take a short break for five minutes. This small rest allows your mind to refresh. After completing a cycle of four Pomodoros, you are rewarded with a longer break of 15 to 30 minutes.

The secret to its success is that it changes how we perceive time. Instead of seeing time as an enemy that is running out, the technique teaches us to work with time. By breaking down large projects into small, manageable intervals, it reduces anxiety and prevents burnout. Today, it remains one of the most popular ways to improve productivity and maintain focus in a busy world.
