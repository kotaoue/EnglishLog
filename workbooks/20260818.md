## テーマ: The Epistemology of "Post-Truth" and the Breakdown of Consensus Reality

### 英文パッセージ

The contemporary discourse surrounding the "post-truth" era often focuses on the proliferation of misinformation, yet the phenomenon represents a deeper epistemological crisis: the erosion of consensus reality. In a functioning democracy, while citizens may disagree on the interpretation of facts, they traditionally operated within a shared framework of empirical evidence. However, the current digital landscape, characterized by algorithmic curation and echo chambers, has facilitated a shift from "what is true" to "what feels true," a tendency described as "truthiness."

This shift is not merely a byproduct of technological flaws but is rooted in the psychological mechanism of motivated reasoning. Individuals are cognitively predisposed to filter information that challenges their pre-existing worldviews while uncritically accepting data that reinforces their identity. When this cognitive bias is amplified by social media platforms—whose business models prioritize engagement over veracity—the result is a fragmented public sphere where objective truth is no longer a prerequisite for political mobilization.

Furthermore, the "post-truth" condition exploits the inherent vulnerability of pluralistic societies. While the democratization of information was intended to empower the marginalized, it has also been weaponized to sow systemic doubt. By flooding the information ecosystem with contradictory narratives, malicious actors do not necessarily aim to make people believe a specific lie; rather, they aim to induce a state of "epistemic exhaustion." In this state, the public becomes so overwhelmed by the difficulty of discerning fact from fiction that they eventually abandon the pursuit of objective truth altogether, succumbing to cynicism or tribalism.
