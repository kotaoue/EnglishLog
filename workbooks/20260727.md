## テーマ: The Ethics of Affective Computing and Emotional Surveillance

### 英文パッセージ

Affective computing, the interdisciplinary field concerned with systems that can recognize, interpret, and simulate human affects, has transitioned from a theoretical niche to a pervasive commercial reality. By leveraging biometric sensors and computer vision, algorithms can now discern micro-expressions and physiological cues to infer a user’s internal state. While proponents champion its utility in mental healthcare—where it can identify early signs of depression—and in customer experience optimization, the rapid deployment of these technologies has outpaced ethical discourse, leading to the rise of "emotional surveillance."

The core of the controversy lies in the inherent reductionism of current AI models. Many systems rely on outdated psychological theories suggesting that human emotions are universal and easily categorized. This overlooks the nuanced, socio-cultural contexts that shape emotional expression, potentially leading to discriminatory outcomes in high-stakes environments like recruitment or law enforcement. Furthermore, the commercialization of affect creates a "privacy of the mind" dilemma. If our subconscious physiological responses are harvested and monetized without explicit comprehension of the implications, we risk entering an era where the last bastion of human autonomy—our private feelings—is commodified. To mitigate these risks, a shift toward "affective transparency" is essential, necessitating robust regulatory frameworks that distinguish between beneficial emotional assistance and invasive psychological profiling.
