## テーマ: The "Dead Internet Theory" and the Rise of AI-Generated Content

### 英文パッセージ

The "Dead Internet Theory" is a fascinating, though somewhat controversial, concept that suggests the majority of internet traffic and content is no longer created by humans. Instead, proponents of this theory argue that bots, algorithms, and artificial intelligence have taken over the digital landscape. While the extreme version of this theory—that the internet "died" around 2010 and is now entirely automated—is largely considered a conspiracy, the underlying concerns are becoming increasingly relevant in the age of generative AI.

In the past, the internet was a place for genuine human connection and grassroots creativity. However, the rise of Large Language Models (LLMs) has made it easier than ever to produce massive amounts of text, images, and videos with a single click. This shift has led to an "engagement-driven" environment where algorithms prioritize content that generates clicks, often at the expense of quality or truth. As a result, users may find themselves interacting with sophisticated bots on social media without even realizing it.

This phenomenon creates a "feedback loop" where AI-generated content is used to train future AI models. If the internet becomes saturated with synthetic data, the distinction between human thought and machine output may eventually vanish. For readers, the challenge is to develop a higher level of digital literacy to navigate this evolving landscape. Understanding the source of information is no longer just a skill; it is a necessity for maintaining a clear view of reality.
