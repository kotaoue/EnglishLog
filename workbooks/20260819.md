## テーマ: The Ship of Theseus and the Ethics of Mind Uploading

### 英文パッセージ

The Ship of Theseus, a thought experiment dating back to ancient Greece, poses a fundamental question about identity: if every plank of a wooden ship is replaced one by one, does the vessel remain the same ship? This paradox finds a modern, high-stakes parallel in the conceptual frontier of "mind uploading"—the hypothetical process of scanning the human brain and recreating its neural architecture in a digital substrate.

Proponents of transhumanism often embrace "patternism," the view that identity resides not in the biological matter of the brain, but in the intricate configuration of neural connections and informational processing. From this perspective, an exact digital replica of one’s mind would constitute a continuation of the self, effectively granting a form of digital immortality. However, this proposition invites profound ontological skepticism. If the biological original persists after the digital copy is created, which one is the "true" individual? Does identity allow for bifurcation, or does the act of copying merely create a sophisticated simulation?

Furthermore, the transition from biological wetware to silicon-based hardware raises questions about the subjective experience of consciousness. If consciousness is inextricably tied to the specific physiological processes of the human body, a digital emulation might be nothing more than a "philosophical zombie"—a system that mimics human behavior perfectly without any internal sentience. As we edge closer to neurotechnologies that blur the line between organic and synthetic intelligence, the Ship of Theseus ceases to be a mere academic riddle and becomes a crucible for defining the future of human existence.
