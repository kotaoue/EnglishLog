## テーマ: The Fermi Paradox and the "Great Filter" Hypothesis

### 英文パッセージ

The Fermi Paradox highlights the startling discrepancy between the high statistical probability of extraterrestrial civilizations and the complete lack of evidence for their existence. Given the billions of stars in our galaxy similar to the Sun, many of which are billions of years older, it stands to reason that technologically advanced civilizations should have emerged and colonized the Milky Way by now. Yet, the cosmos remains profoundly silent—a phenomenon known as the "Great Silence."

To reconcile this contradiction, the "Great Filter" hypothesis suggests that there is a formidable evolutionary hurdle or existential catastrophe that prevents life from reaching the stage of interstellar colonization. This filter could be positioned at any point in a civilization's development. If the filter is behind us—meaning the transition from prokaryotic to eukaryotic life or the emergence of intelligence is incredibly rare—then humanity may be one of the few, or even the only, species to have surpassed it. 

Conversely, the more chilling possibility is that the Great Filter lies ahead of us. This implies that most civilizations reach a certain level of technological prowess—such as the mastery of nuclear energy, biotechnology, or artificial intelligence—only to inevitably succumb to self-destruction or environmental collapse. In this view, our discovery of complex life on Mars or Europa would be devastating news; it would suggest that the early stages of life are common, thereby increasing the likelihood that the Filter is a future event that awaits us. Consequently, our survival may depend on whether we possess the collective wisdom to navigate the very technologies that threaten to obsolete our biological existence.
