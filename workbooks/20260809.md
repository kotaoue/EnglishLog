## テーマ: The Concept of "Eudaimonia" vs. Hedonia in the Digital Attention Economy

### 英文パッセージ

The contemporary digital landscape is often characterized as an "attention economy," where human focus is the primary commodity. Within this framework, a critical philosophical tension emerges between two classical Greek concepts of happiness: *hedonia* and *eudaimonia*. While the former relates to the pursuit of immediate pleasure, comfort, and sensory gratification, the latter, rooted in Aristotelian ethics, refers to "human flourishing"—the realization of one's potential through virtuous activity and purpose.

Critics argue that the architectural underpinnings of modern social media are inherently skewed toward the promotion of *hedonia*. Through variable reward schedules and dopamine-driven feedback loops, platforms are designed to elicit ephemeral engagement rather than meaningful contemplation. This incessant stream of notifications and "likes" provides a transient hedonic spike but often leaves the user in a state of psychological depletion. Such a design paradigm effectively prioritizes short-term retention over the long-term cultivation of *eudaimonia*, which requires sustained attention, self-reflection, and the capacity for deep work—faculties that are being systematically eroded by fragmented digital environments.

Furthermore, the commodification of attention raises profound ethical questions regarding "cognitive autonomy." If our digital tools are optimized to exploit our evolutionary predispositions for novelty and social validation, the individual's ability to pursue a life of *eudaimonic* purpose becomes compromised. True flourishing is not merely the absence of boredom but the presence of agency in one's intellectual and moral development. Consequently, the challenge for the next generation of technologists and users alike is to transcend the hedonic treadmill of current interface designs and foster digital ecosystems that are conducive to genuine human excellence.
