## テーマ: The Epistemology of Deepfakes and the Erosion of Shared Reality

### 英文パッセージ

The rapid proliferation of hyper-realistic generative AI, particularly in the form of "deepfakes," has transcended mere technical novelty to become a profound ontological threat. Traditionally, the adage "seeing is believing" served as a foundational pillar of human epistemology, providing a reliable, albeit imperfect, mechanism for navigating social and political landscapes. However, the advent of seamless synthetic media threatens to sever the link between sensory perception and factual veracity, potentially ushering in an era of "epistemic nihilism."

In this emerging landscape, the danger is twofold. First is the obvious risk of deception—the fabrication of events that never occurred to manipulate public opinion or incinerate individual reputations. Yet, a more insidious consequence is what scholars term the "liar’s dividend." As the public becomes increasingly cognizant of the ubiquity of synthetic content, the mere possibility of forgery allows bad actors to dismiss authentic evidence of wrongdoing as "fake." This corrosive skepticism undermines the very possibility of a shared reality, which is the prerequisite for any functioning deliberative democracy.

Furthermore, the psychological toll of chronic uncertainty cannot be overstated. When the evidentiary value of audiovisual records is neutralized, society may retreat into "affective polarization," where individuals rely solely on their preconceived biases to determine what is true, rather than objective evidence. Addressing this challenge requires more than just technical detection tools; it demands a radical reimagining of digital literacy and a collective commitment to reconstructing the trust infrastructure that once underpinned our information ecosystems.
